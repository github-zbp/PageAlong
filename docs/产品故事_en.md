## Who I am

My name is Apei. I am a programmer, and I am also the product owner, developer, operator, and promoter of PageAlong. I am glad you came to this page to see the story behind the product.

## Why I built this product

### The story starts with a longer commute

This product came from a very specific situation: I wanted to keep learning during long commutes without reaching for short videos, and I wanted to do it in a lighter, less effortful way.

As a programmer, the so-called "35-year-old crisis" means I keep updating my knowledge. I also enjoy learning new technologies, so after work I often read practical technical articles and study product and operations topics. That made my days feel full.

Things changed after I moved. My commute went from 25 minutes to 75 minutes, so a lot more time was spent on the road and my uninterrupted learning time at home became much shorter. On the subway I would sometimes fall back into scrolling, and by the time I got home I was already tired and would keep scrolling out of habit.

Then one day I remembered a point from a book about time and knowledge management: do not be a perfectionist, and do not rely only on large blocks of time when smaller fragments can also be used for learning. So I began my own experiment with fragmented learning on the subway.

### The workarounds I tried first

At first I sent useful article URLs to WeChat's file transfer assistant, then copied them into a browser the next day to read them. After enough links piled up, going back to one meant scrolling through chat history, which was inconvenient. Looking down at a phone for 50 or 60 minutes was also hard on my neck, shoulders, and eyes. My body could scroll for hours, but it struggled once I asked it to focus on text.

Later I tried listening instead: convert web articles to audio, then send the audio to my phone or cloud drive. That let me study with my eyes closed. I found a text-to-speech tool called TTSMaker. It worked, but it had obvious limits: each conversion could handle only 1,000 Chinese characters, and the weekly limit was 20,000 characters. A single long article had to be copied and pasted several times, and the result became multiple audio files scattered across cloud storage and WeChat.

The problem became clear. I did not just need a read-aloud button. I needed one learning trail that connected the source, the audio, and the point where I stopped.

### Building it myself

Sorry, I am not playing along with those limits anymore. I decided to build my own audio-based learning tool with content management.

Codex and I used our spare time after work for two months to handle product planning, interaction design, market research, competitor research, development, testing, deployment, and brand design. I will also keep taking care of promotion and operations. It was a reminder of how hard independent building was before AI tools existed.

PageAlong now has a Web app, an Android app, and a Chrome extension.

The Android app is for the main mobile use cases: being out, commuting, doing chores, or listening before sleep. It helps people listen to web podcasts or web courses when looking at a screen is inconvenient.
The Web app is for focused study in larger blocks of time: a reader-friendly course page and audio player, notes and organization, five different color themes, and export to PDF, Word, or Markdown.
The Chrome extension is for clipping on desktop: save the current page to PageAlong with one click, instead of copying and pasting, or even listen in the browser without saving first.

Some people ask why the app is only available on Android and not iOS. Apple's App Store developer program costs 99 USD per year, which is expensive for a free project that is still being funded by enthusiasm. I would rather wait until the user base is larger and the budget is more stable before building the iOS app.

## Why it is called PageAlong

I am not a professional brand strategist. "PageAlong" was not my own idea; I asked AI for help. My brief was to stay close to the idea of letting people learn anytime, anywhere, while giving the name a warm feeling.

That is how "PageAlong" came out of AI's quick thinking.

The "page" points to web pages, articles, documents, and course pages. "Along" suggests that the content can move together with the learner. The English name also keeps the feeling of "follow along" and "read along."

The logo idea is simple. This is an audio reading tool, so I imagined "a book wearing headphones." I came up with the concept myself, and AI helped turn it into a concrete image.

## What I hope it can become

My goal for this product is simple: solve my own problem of having a long commute, too little learning time, and a desire to make fragmented learning easier. If the product feels comfortable to use, that is enough for me.

I also want to keep it free for people who have the same problem or who want to spend less time staring at screens. I hope it can improve the way they read or learn.

If people find it useful, I will be happy. If they do not, I still want to hear the feedback.

If the user base grows later, I may commercialize the product to cover server costs and third-party text-to-speech costs. I hope people can understand that.

### What I want to build next

- More voices, including male voices, female voices, and regional accents
- Better-looking book and course covers
- AI-generated notes and summaries, with notes that can also become audio
- A knowledge community and forum so people can share useful content
- Sync with tools such as Notion and Obsidian

These plans only make sense if the user base keeps growing and the comments and replies stay positive. Your feedback and suggestions matter, because they tell me what people actually want and need. That is what keeps me building.
